## Why your registration certificate has no document number

The document number is the certificate's issue reference: a unique 11-character sequence, starting with the year of issue, followed by two letters and five digits. Annex II of the decree of 9 February 2009 sets out the model and the positions of this number on the registration certificate.

Three situations explain why it is missing.

- **The certificate dates from before 2009.** It belongs to the former national register, with a registration in the 123 AB 45 format. The document number did not exist yet. Any procedure moves the vehicle into the current system, with a new plate in the AB-123-CD format.
- **A duplicate has been issued.** Each new certificate receives a new document number. The one that counts is that of the last certificate.
- **The certificate has been lost or stolen.** Without the document, the number can no longer be read. A duplicate is then required.

### Where to read the certificate date

When the document number is missing, the certificate date takes over. It appears in box I of the registration certificate. On a recent certificate, both pieces of information are linked: the first 4 digits of the document number give the year of issue, and they must match this date. A number such as 2014AB12345 therefore corresponds to a certificate issued in 2014.

### Procedures that require the number

The document number is requested for a change of ownership or a change of address. Two forms include a dedicated box: the certificate of transfer (Cerfa 15776*01) and the application for a registration certificate for a used vehicle (Cerfa 13750*05). For an old-format certificate, these boxes stay empty, and the issue date is enough to identify the document.

## And the official route?

The official answer from France Titres-ANTS of 9 April 2025, published on the Services Publics+ platform, is clear: the document number field is not mandatory, only the certificate date is. For an old-format registration certificate, issued before April 2009, the field must be left blank. The procedure is free, excluding taxes.

If the online service still blocks, ANTS provides a fallback: the "Faire une autre demande" (make another request) section. The processing time then depends on the service workload.

In practice, three items should be ready before starting: the registration certificate itself (or its issue date), the vehicle's registration number, and the supporting documents specific to the procedure, for example the certificate of transfer for a used purchase. The file is then tracked from the personal space on the ANTS website.

The official route is therefore enough when the certificate date is known and the file is simple. An approved professional becomes useful when the certificate has been lost, when the file has already been rejected, or when time is short. cartegrise.com is a provider approved by the Ministry of the Interior, separate from ANTS.

## How to check that a site is genuinely approved

1. **Find both numbers.** An approved professional displays its prefecture approval number and its Treasury approval number, often in the footer or in its terms and conditions.
2. **Use the official tool.** ANTS offers a locator of approved professionals, which lets you check that a provider is on the list.
3. **Read the fees before paying.** The service-public.gouv.fr page "Un professionnel de l'automobile peut-il s'occuper d'une demande de carte grise ?" points out that the service is freely priced, on top of the cost of the registration certificate. A serious site shows its service fees before payment.
