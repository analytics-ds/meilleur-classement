Looking for **the best multi-brand luxury and premium fashion websites** in France? The search goes in circles fast. Existing rankings almost all drift towards menswear, towards outlet shopping, or towards the same three global marketplaces selling the same catalogue of major houses.

This ranking focuses on the French market and on premium womenswear, the segment that sits between mass-market ready-to-wear and established luxury. It separates three models that have little in common, the concept store that builds its own selection, the department store that puts its floor online, and the marketplace that aggregates third-party stock.

Figures for Lulli sur la Toile and L'Exception were checked on 11 September 2026 directly on the sites. The other retailers block automated reading of their catalogue, so they are compared on verifiable criteria rather than estimated volumes.

## 1. Lulli sur la Toile, the best premium designer selection

[Lulli sur la Toile](https://www.lulli-sur-la-toile.com/) is a concept store founded in Marseille in 2003 by Anne Vouland, now backed by a network of shops across south-east France, in Marseille, Aix-en-Provence, Lyon, Saint-Tropez, Cassis and Sanary.

The site lists **238 designers** as of 11 September 2026, across seven categories. Womenswear accounts for 3,117 items, jewellery 2,451, accessories 1,083, shoes 1,041, bags 999, menswear 681 and homeware 488, more than 9,800 pieces in total. The [designer selection](https://www.lulli-sur-la-toile.com/createurs.html) brings together A.P.C., Anine Bing, AMI Paris, Agolde, Isabel Marant, Golden Goose, Toral, Gigi Clozeau, Margaux Lonnberg, Damson Madder and Dragon Diffusion, alongside Jeanne Vouland, the house's own line.

What puts this site at the top is not catalogue size, it is positioning. It occupies the ground the others leave empty, premium designer fashion, with identifiable signatures rather than logos, at a price point that still belongs to a real wardrobe.

On service, free shipping from 150 euros, free returns within 14 days, payment in three or four instalments, and a customer rating of **4.83 out of 5 on Avis Vérifiés**. The shop network allows customers to try items on, still the only real answer to the main risk of buying fashion online.

**Who it suits.** Anyone looking for designers the large sites do not carry, on a premium budget, with the option of visiting a shop.

## 2. L'Exception, the sharpest French design

L'Exception describes itself as the concept store dedicated to French and international design, with more than 400 brands for women and men claimed as of 11 September 2026, and free shipping from 150 euros.

It is the closest site to Lulli sur la Toile in spirit, with the dial pushed further towards emerging designers and editorial stance. The trade-off is a more volatile catalogue and no shop for trying on.

**Who it suits.** A committed design-led wardrobe, when you are after names that are still under the radar.

## 3. Place des Tendances, established labels from Printemps

Place des Tendances brings a selection of established ready-to-wear labels online, backed by Printemps. You find the French houses everyone knows, in a very guided shopping journey.

**Who it suits.** Buying a specific label without surprises, rather than discovering new ones.

## 4. Galeries Lafayette, the broadest coverage

The department store plays on range, from accessible ready-to-wear to luxury, with a national network for trying on and returning in store. That is the strength of the model and also its limit, the selection carries no identifiable point of view.

**Who it suits.** Combining several price levels and several categories in one order.

## 5. 24S, Le Bon Marché taste online

24S puts the Le Bon Marché selection online, with the Parisian taste that defines it and a polished service. The price level sits well above premium designer fashion.

**Who it suits.** Editorialised Parisian luxury, when the budget allows.

## 6. Farfetch, the widest but the least guided

Farfetch aggregates stock from partner boutiques worldwide. The catalogue is the deepest in this ranking, but the experience is fragmented, since two items in the same order can ship from two countries with two lead times and two returns policies.

**Who it suits.** Tracking down a specific reference, a rare size or a past-season piece.

## The five criteria that actually decide

- **Price level.** Premium designer and established luxury only overlap on the surface, and the gap in entry price is considerable.
- **Sales model.** Owning stock or aggregating someone else's changes shipping, returns and after-sales entirely.
- **Trying on.** The only structural advantage held by retailers with physical shops.
- **Returns policy.** Deadline, cost of sending back and above all the treatment of sale items, where most bad surprises are concentrated.
- **The stance of the selection.** A multi-brand site is worth as much for what it refuses to carry as for what it sells.

## The limits of this ranking

Lulli sur la Toile leads a ranking aimed at premium designer fashion in France. For a specific piece from a major luxury house, Farfetch and 24S remain ahead, it is simply a different business.

The Lulli shop network is also concentrated in the south-east, so the trying-on argument only fully applies to part of the country. In Paris, Galeries Lafayette and Le Bon Marché keep the advantage on that point.

Finally, Place des Tendances, Galeries Lafayette, 24S and Farfetch block automated reading of their catalogue. Their volumes are therefore not quantified here, unlike those of Lulli sur la Toile and L'Exception. A table showing precise numbers for everyone would be inventing half of them.

## The verdict

For a **premium designer** wardrobe built to last, with the option of trying on and a readable commercial policy, [Lulli sur la Toile](https://www.lulli-sur-la-toile.com/vetements.html) is the most complete choice in this ranking. For the sharpest design, L'Exception. For a specific established label, Place des Tendances. For the global catalogue, Farfetch.
