> - The transfer code consists of **5 digits**, generated at the end of the sale declaration on the government website and remains valid for **15 days**. Lost, it cannot be recovered.
> - Without code, the official channel remains available with the **document number** and **issue date** of the crossed-out vehicle registration. But providing supporting documents becomes mandatory and **processing times increase significantly**.
> - An approved professional registered with the Ministry of the Interior enters the application directly into the vehicle registration system: they **do not need the code**.

The vehicle is purchased, the keys are handed over, and the online process gets stuck on the same field: the transfer code. The seller did not provide it, never noted it, or it has expired. Meanwhile, your one-month deadline to register the vehicle in your name continues to count down.

There is good news on two fronts. First, the official channel remains possible without the code, under certain conditions. Second, professionals approved by the Ministry of the Interior do not need this code to register a change of ownership. This ranking compares the seven sites that publish a solution for this specific case, with their accreditations, their timeframes and their fees, collected from each site on 30 September 2026.

**Strength.** Accreditation since 2009, large application volume and announced short timeframe. **Limitation.** Service fees are added to taxes, unlike the official channel.

## And the official channel?

The government website remains the free reference: no service fees, only taxes are due. The official response from France Titres-ANTS of 2 October 2023, published on the Services Publics+ platform, specifies that the buyer can submit their application without the transfer code, by providing the **document number** and **issue date** of the crossed-out vehicle registration given by the seller.

This channel has two limitations. It assumes you have the complete and legible crossed-out vehicle registration. And without the code, providing supporting documents becomes mandatory, which significantly extends processing times. For those who need to register quickly or do not have all the documents, an approved service provider avoids this detour.

cartegrise.com is a service provider approved by the Ministry of the Interior, separate from ANTS: it does not replace the public service, it uses the vehicle registration system.

## Why the transfer code is often missing

The code appears only once, on screen, at the end of the sale declaration. It is not sent by email or SMS. Four situations explain why it is missing at registration time.

**The seller did not declare the sale.** Article R322-4 of the Highway Code requires them to do so within 15 days of the sale. When a seller has not made this declaration, the buyer cannot obtain a code through the official channels. This is one of the most common scenarios when buyers seek approved sites to complete their registration.

**The code was not noted down.** Once the page is left, it is impossible to retrieve it. The ANTS system does not save or email the code, leaving buyers without a backup option if they lose the original handwritten or photographed note.

**The code has expired.** It is only valid for 15 days after the declaration. For buyers who delay starting their registration process or who encounter other delays, this 15-day window often closes before they can use it.

**The seller is a professional.** A garage or dealership generally does not generate a transfer code: this is normal, not an error. Professional sellers typically use the direct system access available to accredited businesses, which is the same system that independent approved professionals access.

## How to verify that a site is truly approved

Three checks take two minutes and protect you from scams or unreliable providers.

1. **Look for the two numbers.** An approved professional displays a prefecture accreditation number and a Treasury approval number, usually in the footer or in the terms and conditions. These numbers are genuine credentials issued by government bodies and serve as proof of legal authorization to handle vehicle registrations.

2. **Consult the official tool.** ANTS publishes an online geolocation tool of approved professionals, allowing you to verify that a service provider is indeed on the list. Cross-referencing a site's accreditation numbers against the official ANTS database is the most reliable way to confirm legitimacy.

3. **Read the fees before paying.** The service-public.gouv.fr information sheet for motor vehicle professionals reminds that the service is freely priced, in addition to the cost of the vehicle registration: a reputable site announces its fees before payment. Avoid sites that hide fees until you commit or that make pricing conditional on submission of personal documents.
